# Design System: Η εβδομάδα μου · ΤΗΜΜΥ

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Week in the Studio"**

The student's own timetable is a product shot. It sits as matte satin 3D slabs on a board standing on a ground plane in a cool-grey photo studio, lit like an object on a seamless backdrop, and the planner sheet rises over it like a page slid onto the set. Everything around the object stays quiet: cool studio greys, ink type, one neon accent. The colour lives in the courses themselves. The first screen is composed as a poster: the title sits at the top left at poster size, in a light weight, on the same left edge as the sheet's content; the week stands right of its short second line and from half-way down its long first one, out past the right edge and down to the sheet, whose edge cuts it; and the week's facts and the one action sit at the foot, just above the sheet's edge. The grounds are flat: the studio is one tone and the sheet another, meeting at a seam, with no glow behind the board and no shadow on the sheet's edge. The page closes where it began, on a black field from edge to edge.

Below the fold the studio becomes a calm, editorial work surface. The week is the canvas and is never cropped sideways. Pills for everything you press, soft 20-28px surfaces, 12px event blocks (a tinted card with a colour bar on paper, a flat field of colour in the dark; the 3D tile is the object, the block is its print), giant light grotesk numerals for the stats. The signature move is a scroll morph: on desktop and tablet the 3D board swings to a top-down view and lands exactly on the planner's week, and the slabs become its flat blocks. The tiles are objects and the board is their tray: whenever the week changes place or content, the tiles leave the tray and are dealt back Monday to Friday (by day, then start time), 40ms apart and the whole deal held to about half a second, with one landing. In 3D a tile comes in from the viewer's side of the glass, large and close, and shrinks onto its slot, accelerating under gravity, and squashes as it lands; on the 2D week a block arrives a touch larger and presses down to size. The intro, the hop back after a spin, the return after a throw, the scroll down onto the planner, new blocks and the season switch all deal this way. Elsewhere motion is physical and springy: digits roll, the season thumb settles with a small overshoot, a block that stays on the week is the same element and slides to its new slot or size (300ms, on the spring, never rebuilt), and a course flies on an arc into its slot, swelling toward the viewer on the way. Exits are shorter than entrances and leave the way things came, on the ease-out: dialogs drop and fade, phone sheets slide back down, a removed block lifts away where it stood, the update toast lifts away. The one exception: a removed block's opacity runs on the CSS `ease` keyword so the fade stays readable, while its scale stays on the ease-out. A block that has just started to clash pulses its outline once on the there-and-back curve. Every one of these collapses to an instant, static path under reduced motion.

The world is modelled on butter.video, chosen by the user. Rejected and not to return: the cool-grey, electric-blue, pastel-course "premium product app" world set in Commissioner with 16/10 radii.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Cool studio greys with a faint blue cast and an off-white (light) or ink (dark) planner sheet.
- Ink is the primary action colour; hot pink is a single neon note, never a fill for large surfaces.
- Two course families and a slate, cobalts and ambers plus one slate, told apart by lightness before hue so no course relies on red against green; ink text on a tinted card in light, ink or white by contrast on a flat field in dark.
- Geologica everywhere, tabular figures by default, light weights at display sizes (hero title 350, numerals 300) with tight negative tracking.
- Pill controls, soft rectangles for surfaces, 12px for event blocks.
- Surfaces part by a step of tone, not by lines or resting shadows; only things that really float (dialogs, menus, drawers, tooltips, the rail button, the toast) cast a shadow. Event blocks are flat: a tint with a colour bar in light, a field of colour in dark. The calendar grid keeps its lines.
- A poster first screen: the title at the top left at poster size on the sheet's left edge, the 3D week right of its short second line and out past the right edge, the facts and the one action at the foot. The studio and the sheet are two flat tones meeting at a seam, with no gradient, glow or shadow between them.
- The page closes on a black field in both themes, where the title is rebuilt from blocks in the week's own colours.
- The hero's 3D week and the planner's 2D week are one object: scrolling morphs one into the other.
- One landing: tiles and blocks are dealt Monday to Friday, 40ms apart, the whole deal held to about half a second; a 3D tile closes from the viewer's side of the glass under gravity and squashes, a 2D block presses from 1.1 to size.
- Motion has depth along the line of sight: things arrive from, and leave toward, the viewer's side of the glass (a tile shrinking onto its slot, a throw that comes at the lens, a dialog coming forward out of the page, a headline standing up toward the reader), and nothing just slides in the screen plane; it always resolves to the flat, exact planner.
- Three curves: the spring (~5% overshoot) for things that settle into a new value or slot, the ease-out for transitions, presses and exits, the there-and-back in-out for one-shot pulses; everything static under reduced motion.

## Colors

A cool-grey studio with one neon voice; colour is reserved for courses.

### Primary
- **Neon Hot Pink** (`neon-pink`, dark `dark-neon-pink`): the headline's and the sign-off's full stop, today's day pill (the only mark on today's column head: there is no column wash and no head tint), the now line (a 2px rule across today's column with a 7px dot at its left edge) and the 6px dot before the hero's now/next line, the course "add" hover (fine pointers only), the rail button's count badge, link hover underlines, selection and caret. Text on it is always ink (`on-accent`). Its hover step is `neon-pink-hover`.

### Neutral
- **Studio Grey** (`studio-grey` / `dark-studio-grey`): the page's own ground, under the hero and the sheet. Every neutral, light and dark, leans slightly cool (a few points of blue over red, ink included); none is tinted warm.
- **Studio Floor** (`studio-floor` / `dark-studio-floor`): the hero's flat ground, one tone for the whole studio behind the 3D board; the sheet meets it at a seam, with no gradient, glow or shadow between them.
- **Close** (`close` / `dark-close`): the black field the page ends on, edge to edge in both themes, carrying light type (`#f2f2f2`, `#bdbdbd` and `#a3a3a3` for text, its second step and meta); the sign-off's letters are cut out of their blocks in this colour.
- **Planner Sheet** (`planner-sheet` / `dark-planner-sheet`): the sheet that rises over the hero; also the text colour on ink pills.
- **Surfaces** (`surface`, `surface-2`, `surface-3` and dark counterparts): raised cards, dialogs and menus (`surface`); input wells, callouts, option rows, the checks panel and the empty state (`surface-2`); tags, skeleton peaks and hovered option rows (`surface-3`).
- **Fills** (`--fill`, `--fill-2`: ink at 7% / 12%, 9% / 15% in dark): the resting and hovered fill of every default and quiet control, the season track, the count chip and the add buttons, so a control is a step of ink over whatever it sits on.
- **Hairlines** (`hairline`, `hairline-strong`): the calendar's day columns, hour rules (at 38%) and day-head edge, input and select wells, and unchecked swatches. Nothing else is divided by a line. Every other hour of the week grid is a band of ink at 2% (3% in dark), a step of tone rather than a line; print drops the bands.
- **Ink** (`ink`, `ink-2`, `ink-3`): text, primary buttons, the brand mark, the season thumb, focus rings. Ink-3 is the floor for meta text.

### Course palette
Two families and a slate, told apart by lightness before hue. Cobalts: `#4a6cf0`, `#a9c1ff`, `#2b3f9e`, `#d7e3ff`. Ambers: `#f0b23c`, `#c98a26`, `#ffe08f`, `#e09a2e`. Slate: `#6f7a92`. They are assigned per course by default in the slot order cobalt, amber, light cobalt, deep amber, slate, pale amber, deep cobalt, mid amber, pale cobalt, so neighbouring slots alternate family and step through dark and pale; a slot already taken among the selected courses walks on to the next free one. The owner is red-green colour-blind, so no course relies on red against green. Hot pink is the single accent and is not a course colour. In light mode a block is a printed timetable card: 30% of the hue mixed over `surface`, a 4px inset bar of the full hue down its left edge, and ink text. In dark mode a block is a flat field of the hue mixed 90% with the surface, with text in ink or white, whichever gives higher WCAG contrast against that mix. Either way there is no lip, rim, inner edge or resting shadow. In the 3D hero the tile's top is the course colour itself under a matte satin finish and no tone curve, lit so it reproduces the planner's colour within about 2 degrees of hue; as the board lands, each slab top shifts to the block's flat fill (the course colour, mixed 10% into the surface in dark) so the hand-over is seamless. The code engraved in a tile is inked by the same contrast rule as the block text. An empty week shows chrome sample slabs.

### Semantic
- **Success** (passed course), **Warning** (needs review), **Danger** (overlap). Each has a soft companion at 7-12% alpha for callout fills.

### Named Rules
**The One Neon Rule.** Hot pink is the only accent and it appears as small marks (the headline's and sign-off's full stops, today's day pill and its tab underline on phones, the now line and its dot, the hero's now/next dot and countdown prime, a count, a hover), never as a button fill, a column wash or a course colour. Primary actions are ink.

**The Colour Is the Course Rule.** Colour belongs to the course palette. Chrome stays grey and ink, and the one accent is pink. The palette is cobalts and ambers with a single slate; it has no green, no red and no pink, so a course can never be mistaken for a state or for the accent, and none depends on a red-green difference. Courses are told apart by lightness before hue. A selected rail row borrows its course's hue as a 16% wash, the one place chrome takes a course colour, so the list and the week read as one.

**The Never Colour Alone Rule.** Every semantic state carries a second signal: passed is a filled green swatch plus a check icon; review is a dashed amber outline plus a hatched strip; overlap is a solid red outline plus a warning icon. A course with no lab in the timetable is a dashed pill or ring in the block's own text colour, never a hue, with the words in the pill, the tooltip and the label.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Geologica (self-hosted Greek and Latin subsets, variable 300-800), with Noto Sans and system-ui as fallback (Noto Sans is only named in the stack; it is not bundled)
**Body Font:** Geologica
**Label/Mono Font:** Geologica; a monospace face appears only in the transfer-code textarea

**Character:** One geometric grotesk with Greek built in, used at every size. Display sizes run in light weights (the headline at 350, the numerals at 300) and are tightly tracked; working sizes stay plain and small, with 500 to 600 for headings and names, so the size of the numbers and the headline does the shouting, not their weight.

- **Title** (500, 20px, 1.25, -0.025em): panel headings (course rail, weekly schedule).

- **Event title** (600, 13px, 1.25): course names inside blocks (14px on the phone agenda); time lines 12px 600, dimmed a step below the title per hue (never below 4.5:1), rooms and teachers 12px. Narrow lanes set the course code at 12px weight 400, step it to 11px at 60px or less (the one place text goes under 12px, because the lane has no room) and run it down the lane at 34px or less.

- **Label** (600, 12px): tags, badges, counters, day-head meta, hour axis (400). Sentence case, never uppercase. The brand mark's two-line monogram (11px 700) is a logo, not text, and is the only other exception to the 12px floor.
- **Print ramp** (print only): title 22px (followed by the season and the data's date at 13px in ink-3), stats 20px, day names 11px, event titles 9px, event times, rooms and day meta 8px. Teachers are dropped.

### Named Rules
**The Numbers Are the Headline Rule.** Figures are tabular everywhere, and the week's totals are set at display scale, not tucked into small chips.

**The One Family Rule.** Geologica carries every role; hierarchy comes from size, weight and tracking, never from a second display face.

## Layout

A week-first canvas inside a sheet. The hero is a full-viewport sticky studio (100svh, min 600px; 92svh on phones); the planner sheet overlaps it by 13svh (9svh on phones) and carries everything else. Content tracks a 2080px maximum with fluid 2vw margins: one token, `--gutter` (at least 20px, 16px on phones), is the page margin of the hero copy, the sheet's padding and the footer's bleed.

- **Desktop (>1180px):** a 340px sticky course rail beside the week, parted from it by 32px of space. On wide layouts the rail can be hidden so the week runs full-width, with the floating rail button to bring it back.
- **Tablet (861-1180px):** the week takes the full width; the rail becomes a 340px side drawer floating 12px from the edges. From 961px the open drawer pushes the week aside rather than covering it, so an added course is seen landing, and pressing or focusing the week leaves the drawer open; below 961px it overlays. It slides in over 500ms and out in 200ms, and tabbing out of it closes it.
- **Phone (<=860px):** the week becomes a vertical agenda showing one day at a time under the day tabs (see Day tabs), with sticky day headings in the all-days list; the rail becomes a bottom sheet (84dvh, grab handle, scrim; up in 500ms, down in 200ms) that a finger can drag down from its handle or heading, closing past 80px or on a quick downward flick (over 0.5px/ms at release) and otherwise springing back; dialogs become bottom sheets; the sheet corners drop to the phone sheet radius. During the teaching period, on a weekday with courses chosen, the hero's "Στο πρόγραμμα" button lands on today's heading in the agenda instead of the top of the planner (smooth where Lenis runs, instant under reduced motion), stopping 12px clear of the header like any anchor; elsewhere it goes to the planner's top. Agenda cards sit 10px apart with 14px titles, and the agenda's bottom padding keeps the last block clear of the floating rail button.
- A floating ink "Μαθήματα" pill with the selected count opens the rail from anywhere once the planner is in view.
- **First screen:** the hero copy is a flex column between the header and the sheet, from 5svh under the header bar (2svh on phones) down to 36px above the sheet's edge (26px on phones): the title at the top, the facts and the one action pushed to the foot. On wide screens the 3D week fills the free space right of the title's short second line; on phones it stands between the title and the facts at about 92% of the screen's width.

Day headings (name, meta, today pill) are centred in their columns and reserve exactly two lines of meta (2.7em) so every day name aligns. Today's meta puts "Σήμερα" on its own line above "N συναντήσεις · N ώρες". On the phone agenda, headings are a left-aligned row and the word runs inline with a comma. Phones and print drop the two-line reservation.

Spacing runs on 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 22 / 28px; panels breathe at 22px vertical padding, the hour grid is 56px per hour, and the header bar is 76px (68px on phones).

### Named Rules
**The No Sideways Week Rule.** The week never scrolls horizontally. The grid is a 54px hour axis (headed Ώρα) plus five equal day columns, whatever they hold. During the teaching period today's column carries a now line, drawn above the hour rules and behind the blocks, moved each minute without re-rendering. Its dot is the part that never hides: it stands in the 7px gutter the blocks leave on the column's left edge, above the hour axis and the blocks (z-index 5, over the axis's 4), so the time stays readable even when a lesson spans the whole column. The classes running now and the next one are marked on the blocks themselves (see Event Block). The first time it is seen in a page view it arrives (also on touch, off under reduced motion): the dot pops from nothing on a spring (400ms, bounce 0.35), then 150ms later the line draws left to right over 600ms on the ease-out. It is hidden in the phone agenda and in print. Parallel lessons split their day into lanes, and narrow lanes step down by container width. At 112px or less a lane shows one head line, start time then course code (it wraps only when both do not fit), and the name below it (a now or next label keeps its dot and loses its word), set compact with soft hyphens at Greek syllable breaks; wider lanes never hyphenate and wrap whole words. At 96px or less, and for lessons of an hour or less, the name goes and the head line stays. At 60px or less it shows the code only (a block marked now or next keeps a lone dot in place of its time). At 50px or less the shared ECE prefix drops and only the course number remains. The full name always stays in the tooltip and dialog. Margin notes move beside the week only when the busiest day still gets 150px per lane. There the checks are a sticky margin note on a `surface-2` panel that mirrors the rail, always open, with their summary as a heading rather than a toggle (out of the tab order).

**The Print Is Its Own Layout Rule.** Print is one A4 landscape sheet in the selected theme: chrome hidden, the sheet and blocks exactly as on screen (light or dark), no today marking (no now line, time pill, hour bands, today tint or now/next label on the blocks), the title followed by the season and the data's date, the week scaled to fit and forced fully visible, the print type ramp, all motion, shadows and focus rings off. Nothing on the printed page sizes itself with viewport units, because Chrome's print preview resolves them against the browser window and a window-tall page spills a blank second sheet.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat at rest: controls, callouts, rows, panels and event blocks part by tone, with no shadow and no outline. Only surfaces that really float take the soft layered shadows and a 1px top highlight in light mode; on ink, where shadows vanish, a hairline marks their edge instead. The planner sheet over the hero casts none: the studio and the sheet are two flat tones that meet at a seam, with no top line, shadow, gradient or glow, and the sheet's rounded top corners are its whole edge. Real depth lives in the hero, where a lit 3D board stands on a ground plane. The light is white and the same in both themes: one key from the upper left with a soft but defined shadow, and a faint white rim; no coloured light, so no reflection tints a course colour. The ground carries a soft pool of light (55% in light, 16% in dark, where a shadow alone would vanish into the page), a large soft shadow thrown away from the key, and a tight contact shadow under the board's outline; the shadow cast on the floor is 20% in light mode and 50% in dark mode. The board is matte (no metal, no coat) in `#f4f5f7` in light mode and `#1d1e21` in dark mode, with hairline edge lines. The 3D stage is its own fixed layer above the planner sheet. It is cut away over the sheet's toolbar and stats band and over the docked rail, so the board slides under the planner's chrome; the band's lower edge is a 20px fade rather than a hard line, so slabs surface from under the toolbar. There is no glass: the header is a plain bar and the hero tooltip a solid `surface` card with the lifted shadow. The dialog backdrop is the one frost (8px blur).

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Lifted** (`0 12px 28px -12px #0e0e0e2e, 0 2px 6px -2px #0e0e0e14`): hovered event blocks, the hero tooltip, a block in flight.
- **Floating** (`0 32px 64px -24px #0e0e0e40, 0 6px 16px -6px #0e0e0e17`): dialogs, menus, the rail drawer, the rail button, the update toast, and the shadow a dealt block presses down from.
- **Highlight** (`inset 0 1px 0 #ffffffcc`): paired with floating surfaces.

### Named Rules
**The Press, Don't Lift Rule.** Controls press (scale 0.96 on active) and never rise on hover. Only event blocks lift on hover (3px and scale 1.02 over 180ms on the ease-out, to the lifted shadow, with a pointer tilt; see Event Block), as the pieces of the week itself, and only where a fine pointer can hover ((hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)) and motion is allowed, so a tap never leaves a block stuck raised.

**The Rings Inside Rule.** Review and highlight outlines on event blocks sit inside the block (negative offset), never outside it. Two rings sit outside: the conflict outline (2px, offset 1px) and keyboard focus: the 2px ink outline offset 2px, raised above the neighbouring lanes. A block with a state ring keeps it, and its focus ring is a shadow outside the block (a 2px sheet gap, then 2px ink), so a focused clash still shows red.

**The Hairline in the Dark Rule.** In dark mode every floating surface (dialog, menu, tooltip, drawer) gets a 1px hairline edge, since shadows cannot read on ink.

## Shapes

Two shape families and nothing between them: everything pressable or countable is a full pill (999px), including buttons, inputs, selects, tags, badges, swatches, the season track and the brand mark; containers are soft rectangles stepping 28px (planner sheet, dialogs; the phone sheet radius on phones, including the rail bottom sheet), 20px (menus, drawer, empty state), 14px (callouts, tooltips, option rows), 10px (menu items). Event blocks sit at 12px, both on the 2D week and as rounded 3D slabs. Course state swatches are 12px circles: filled when selected, a thick ring when some meetings are hidden, dashed when under review or when the course's lab is missing from the program. Soft rectangles take `corner-shape: squircle` where the browser supports it; pills and dots stay round.

### Event Block (signature)
The course as the print of its 3D tile: a 12px block that in light mode reads as a printed timetable on paper (a 30% tint of the course hue over `surface`, a 4px inset bar of the full hue down the left edge, with the content stepping 13px in from it in lanes wider than 112px, and ink text) and in dark mode is a flat field of the hue mixed 90% with the surface, with no lip, rim, inner edge or shadow at rest in either theme; a head line on top, the time (12px 600, and in narrow lanes the course code beside it), then the title, then room and teachers at 12px (co-teachers joined by commas), in the block's text colour. The time and code sit a step back from the title: in light mode they are ink-2 at full strength; in dark mode they are dimmed as far as the hue allows and still keep 4.5:1 on the fill, never past 75% (a hue with no room stays at full strength). A course with no lab in the timetable carries a dashed "Χωρίς εργαστήριο" pill at the foot of each of its blocks (12px weight 500, 1.5px dash in the block's own text colour), which shrinks to a small dashed ring in blocks narrower than 148px, where the words do not fit; its tooltip names it and says hours and overlaps do not count the missing lab yet. Where a fine pointer can hover (the desktop and tablet grid, not the phone agenda), hover lifts it 3px and scales it to 1.02 over 180ms on the ease-out, to the lifted shadow, and tilts it up to 4deg about each axis so the edge under the pointer comes toward the viewer (perspective 700px, on a spring of stiffness 300 and damping 30). Lift and scale use the individual `translate` and `scale` properties and the tilt uses `transform`, so the deal, flight and clear-away animations compose with them; touch and reduced motion never lift or tilt. A press scales it to 0.985, gentler than the controls' 0.96 because blocks are wide. Keyboard focus draws the 2px ink outline offset 2px outside the block, above its neighbours; a block with a state ring keeps that ring, and its focus ring is drawn outside as a shadow (a 2px sheet gap, then 2px ink), so a focused clash still shows red. Review and highlight rings are drawn inside the block's edge, so packed lanes never overlap their neighbours: review is a 2px dashed amber ring plus a 10px hatched strip down the right edge, kept clear of the text. Conflict is one signal in one colour: a 2px solid danger outline set 1px outside the block, plus a warning icon, with no inner ring and no second colour. Hovering a rail row rings every block of that course with a 3px ink ring. The conflict trace pulses its red ring inward (a 3px ring, offset -3px to -6px). When an added course creates a clash, only the block that was already on the week pulses, once and inward (ring offset -2px to -6px and back over 400ms on the there-and-back curve), timed to the newcomer's landing (600ms plus stagger after a flight, 150ms plus stagger after a dealt block); the newcomer never pulses. A removed block lifts away where it stood (180ms: the fade on a plain ease so it stays readable, a 1.04 scale on the ease-out), on the desktop grid only, and not when a neighbour widens into its lane or when the week becomes empty; a season switch lifts every old block away this way. ### Hero Week (scroll morph)
The morph follows the scroll on a critically damped spring rather than 1:1: a slow scroll is tracked closely, while a fast or back-and-forth one plays at the motion's own pace (about four tenths of a second end to end, no reversal on a jitter) and always ends where the page is. The crossfade to the planner's own blocks is eased too, so it never pops when a deal or a throw ends.

A Three.js board (matte `#f4f5f7` or `#1d1e21`, with hairline edges) carrying the student's courses as matte satin slabs, or chrome slabs while the week is empty. Its five equal day columns match the DOM grid. The lens is wide in the hero (a 36 degree field of view, so near slabs loom and far ones fall away) and closes to 24 degrees as the board lands on the planner, the camera backing off to match: a dolly zoom, so the board keeps its size while its perspective drains out, and the landing stays exact. In the hero the camera is framed to the poster: on wide screens the board's free box starts 2% of the width past the end of the title's short second line and half-way down its long first line (on screen the board is a diamond, so its top corner stands clear of that line's end), runs out past the right edge (to 4% beyond it) and down to 95% of the hero's height, so the sheet's edge cuts the board, and the board's radius fills 70% of that box; on phones the board is fitted by its projected outline, not a bounding circle, to about 92% of the screen's width (as high as the space between the title and the facts allows, its top corner free to rise beside the title's short last line) and placed 45% of the way down that free space. Slab height encodes how often a course meets per week: 0.18 world units plus 0.22 per extra weekly meeting, capped at five. A once-a-week course is nearly flat; three or more meetings stand clearly tall. The sample week varies by its own counts. Each real slab has its course code engraved, centred, into its top: a groove inked like the planner's block text (the same contrast rule as the block text colour: near-black on light colours, white on dark ones), matte with no clearcoat, and walls drawn as a normal map from the lettering so they catch the scene's light and shift as the board turns. Narrow tops shrink the code, dropping the ECE prefix last; a top too small stays plain. The tiles are matte satin and are lit so a tile's top reproduces the planner's colour (the renderer applies no tone curve): the faces that turn away from the eye pick up a faint rim of the tile's own colour (strength .2 on every course tile, fading to nothing as the tile flattens into its block, and not on the chrome sample slabs). Three white light cards join the studio before its reflection is baked: a long softbox overhead, which draws a soft streak along every bevel, and two thin strips, one behind and one in front, which cut a hairline highlight down the bevels and give the chrome tiles something sharp to mirror. The board marks hours with lines and half hours with fainter dashed lines; day names shrink to fit their column, never grow. All words on the board (day labels, hours, engraved codes) fade together over e .35-.65, before the planner's own text arrives. Loading: while the data loads, the planner shows shimmering skeletons of its course rows, stats, day heads and blocks; until the 3D board is live, the hero shows a skeleton of the board in its pose, standing where the board will (blocks shimmering on a plane in the surface colour, with no hour lines), which fades as the board rises in, and only where the 3D can load (html.has-3d), so it never waits forever. Rendering budget: while anything moves, the slow drift and conflict pulse included, the board draws on every display refresh (144 times a second on a 144 Hz monitor), with the shadow pass resting while only the drift moves; once the drift has eased out it draws nothing until the next input, and docked under the planner it draws nothing until the page scrolls back to the docking point (a 1px marker just above it comes into view; hidden in print and removed from the HTML backup), a resize or the drawer closing wakes it. It starts in short tasks with shaders compiled asynchronously, and canvases behind uploaded textures are freed. **Material.** The slabs are lit like studio product shots. Their plan outline is a squircle (a quarter superellipse, |x|^4+|y|^4=1, inside the corner radius), matching the 2D squircle corners (Shapes); the rim has smooth normals and a six-segment bevel, so highlights run round the corners. Tops are matte satin (roughness 0.6, ior 1.49, specular .3, under a thin soft coat of .15 at roughness .3), so only the bevels catch a highlight, under a baked studio (512px) with an overhead softbox and two edge strip softboxes, all white, and a 2048 PCF shadow with a soft radius. A tile in conflict glows in the danger colour on its sides and bevels only; its top keeps the course colour. Empty-week sample tiles are mirror chrome (metalness 1, roughness 0.04). The board is matte (roughness .8, no metal, no coat) and stands on a ground plane with a soft light pool and a contact shadow (see Elevation). The class running now, else the next one today (read from the planner's `.is-now` / `.is-next` blocks), glows as lit frosted glass: a rougher body, its course colour pushed halfway to #ff4fa8 as emission, breathing on a roughly 3s cycle (still under reduced motion), with a small tinted point light over it; the glow flattens into its block's fill during the morph, and the breathing never keeps the loop awake, so the sleep budget is unchanged.

**Free view.** The hero board can be explored by hand:
- **Orbit:** the board behaves like a weighted object. Grabbing it catches and stops a spin. While held it follows the hand on a stiff spring, with a slight lag and overshoot. Released, it keeps the hand's speed, soft-limited so a heavy board never whips round: a harder push still goes further, but even a violent flick turns it less than half a turn. It then comes to a firm stop under drag plus a constant grip, in under a second, instead of creeping. Yaw is free. Past its pitch band, tilt stretches like a rubber band and springs back, and the camera never flips over the top.
- **Feel:** while held the board lifts slightly, then drops back on release with one small settle, not a bounce train. It banks into a spin and nods with a tilt. Slabs lean like jelly, more for taller and outer slabs, and squash as the board lands. All of this fades out through the morph. Under reduced motion a drag sets the pose directly: no throw, lift, settle or jelly.
- **Zoom:** pinch or Ctrl+wheel zooms between 0.8x and 1.6x. A plain wheel always scrolls the page.
- **Reset and cursors:** double-click resets the view. The cursor is grab, then grabbing while dragging.
- **Touch:** horizontal swipes spin the board and vertical swipes scroll.
- **Click vs drag:** a 5px threshold separates the two, so a click on a slab still opens the course.
- **Spin too fast:** each slab has a grip of 600 on ω²·(r+1.5)·(1+0.8h), where ω is the spin in rad/s, r the slab's distance from the board's centre and h its height. A slab lets go only while the board is held and spun by hand, after 0.25s continuously over its grip, so the rim and the tall slabs go first and a flick or a released throw never spills anything. Roughly: a brisk drag of about 800px/s keeps every slab, about 1400px/s held for 0.25s spills the rim, and a full-width hard swipe clears the board. A slab that lets go leaves along the spin with a small upward pop, tumbles, and is just fast enough to clear the board's edge. It lands on the studio floor in a ring in view, bounces once, slides to a stop and rolls flat, top up. Slabs on the floor nudge apart rather than overlap. ## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** make primary actions ink pills; keep hot pink to small marks (full stop, today, counts, hover).
- **Do** give every pressable thing a full pill shape and the 0.96 press.
- **Do** let free-view interactions (orbit, zoom) yield to scroll and to the copy: plain wheel scrolls, the board springs home on scroll, and it never covers the headline.
- **Do** pick block text colour by contrast (ink or white) for any course hue, including user-chosen ones, measured against the fill the text sits on (the dark-theme mix in dark mode); in light mode blocks are tinted cards in ink text.
- **Do** pair every success, warning and danger state with an icon, dashes or an outline.
- **Do** use the spring easing (`linear()` with ~5% overshoot, fallback cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)) for things that settle into a new value or slot (digits, the season thumb, a block shifting lanes), the ease-out (cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)) for the 2D press-in and for exits, and the there-and-back curve (cubic-bezier(.77,0,.175,1)) only for one-shot pulses that return to rest; make every animation, view transition, smooth scroll and 3D loop disappear under reduced motion.
- **Do** deal tiles and blocks onto the week Monday to Friday (by day, then start time), 40ms apart and the whole deal held to about half a second (bigger weeks close the gap), with one landing per change: in 3D an arrival from the viewer's side of the glass that accelerates under gravity and ends in a squash, never a spring that overshoots into the board; in 2D a press from scale 1.1 to size about the block's centre, with a fade and the floating shadow coming away as the block lies flat (240ms on the ease-out). While the 3D week stands in for the planner's blocks, the change lands in 3D alone.
- **Do** make exits shorter than their entrances (160-200ms) and send things out the way they came in, on the ease-out; the one exception is a removed block, whose opacity runs on the CSS `ease` keyword so the fade stays readable while its scale stays on the ease-out. For the opposite direction use mirrored keyframes, never `animation-direction: reverse`, which turns the ease-out into an ease-in.
- **Do** keep five equal day columns and step narrow lanes down (time and name, time and code, code, number) rather than widening a busy day.
- **Do** keep the week fully visible without horizontal scroll at every width, and keep print as a dedicated A4 landscape layout.
- **Do** keep the 3D week and the planner's week the same object: same course colours, the same five equal columns and lanes, one hand-over sequence with never two hour axes on screen.
- **Do** use tabular figures and display-scale numerals for the week's totals.
- **Do** keep the first screen a poster: the title at poster size at the top left on the page margin (`--gutter`), the facts and the one action at the foot just above the sheet's edge, the week in the space between and beside them, never behind the copy.
- **Do** keep grounds and blocks flat: the studio and the sheet are two flat tones meeting at a seam, and an event block is a tint of its course colour with a bar (light) or a field of it (dark).

### Don't:
- **Don't** add a second accent colour or fill buttons, panels or headers with neon pink.
- **Don't** use hot pink, green or red as a course colour, or rely on a red against green difference; the palette is cobalts, ambers and one slate.
- **Don't** introduce a second typeface or uppercase tracked labels; Geologica in sentence case carries every role.
- **Don't** use square or small-radius buttons; nothing pressable sits between pill and rectangle.
- **Don't** lift controls on hover or use hard offset shadows; controls press, only event blocks lift.
- **Don't** draw review or highlight rings outside an event block; the conflict outline (1px out) and the focus ring are the only outside rings.
- **Don't** tint the neutrals warm or add a hue to them beyond their faint cool cast; the studio greys stay grey.
- **Don't** put a gradient, glow, shadow or top line between the studio and the sheet, or a lip, rim, inner edge or resting shadow on an event block.
