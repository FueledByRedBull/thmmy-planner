# Design System: Η εβδομάδα μου · ΤΗΜΜΥ

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Week in the Studio"**

The student's own timetable is a product shot. It sits as glossy 3D slabs on a floating board in a neutral photo studio, lit like an object on a seamless backdrop, and the planner sheet rises over it like a page slid onto the set. Everything around the object stays quiet: studio greys, ink type, one neon accent. The colour lives in the courses themselves.

Below the fold the studio becomes a calm, editorial work surface. The week is the canvas and is never cropped sideways. Pills for everything you press, soft 20-28px surfaces, 12px event blocks, giant grotesk numerals for the stats. The signature move is a scroll morph: on desktop and tablet the 3D board swings to a top-down view and lands exactly on the planner's week, and the slabs become its flat blocks. The tiles are objects and the board is their tray: whenever the week changes place or content, the tiles leave the tray and are dealt back Monday to Friday (by day, then start time), 40ms apart and the whole deal held to about half a second, with one landing. In 3D a tile falls, accelerating under gravity, and squashes as it lands; on the 2D week a block arrives a touch larger and presses down to size. The intro, the hop back after a spin, the fall back from the sky, the scroll down onto the planner, new blocks and the season switch all deal this way. Elsewhere motion is physical and springy: digits roll, the season thumb and blocks moving to a new slot settle with a small overshoot, and a course flies on an arc into its slot. Exits are shorter than entrances and leave the way things came, on the ease-out: dialogs drop and fade, phone sheets slide back down, a removed block lifts away where it stood, the update toast lifts away. The one exception: a removed block's opacity runs on the CSS `ease` keyword so the fade stays readable, while its scale stays on the ease-out. A block that has just started to clash pulses its red ring once on the there-and-back curve. Every one of these collapses to an instant, static path under reduced motion.

The world is modelled on butter.video, chosen by the user. Rejected and not to return: the cool-grey, electric-blue, pastel-course "premium product app" world set in Commissioner with 16/10 radii.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Neutral studio greys with an off-white (light) or ink (dark) planner sheet; no tinted neutrals.
- Ink is the primary action colour; hot pink is a single neon note, never a fill for large surfaces.
- Nine vivid course hues, full-strength on event blocks, with ink or white text chosen by contrast.
- Geologica everywhere, tabular figures by default, display sizes with tight negative tracking.
- Pill controls, soft rectangles for surfaces, 12px for event blocks.
- Surfaces part by a step of tone, not by lines or resting shadows; only things that really float (dialogs, menus, drawers, tooltips, the rail button, the sheet over the hero) cast a shadow. The calendar grid keeps its lines.
- The hero's 3D week and the planner's 2D week are one object: scrolling morphs one into the other.
- One landing: tiles and blocks are dealt Monday to Friday, 40ms apart, the whole deal held to about half a second; a 3D tile falls under gravity and squashes, a 2D block presses from 1.06 to size.
- Three curves: the spring (~5% overshoot) for things that settle into a new value or slot, the ease-out for transitions, presses and exits, the there-and-back in-out for one-shot pulses; everything static under reduced motion.

## Colors

A monochrome studio with one neon voice; all saturation is reserved for courses.

### Primary
- **Neon Hot Pink** (`neon-pink`, dark `dark-neon-pink`): the headline's full stop, today's day pill, the today column wash (5% mix), the course "add" hover (fine pointers only), the rail button's count badge, link hover underlines, selection and caret. Text on it is always ink (`on-accent`). Its hover step is `neon-pink-hover`.

### Neutral
- **Studio Grey** (`studio-grey` / `dark-studio-grey`): page ground behind the hero and around the sheet.
- **Studio Floor and Glow** (`studio-floor`, `studio-glow` and dark pair): the hero's vertical gradient and the radial key-light pool behind the 3D board, under a static, neutral film grain (5% light, 7% dark) that only the backdrop carries; the board and the copy stay clean.
- **Planner Sheet** (`planner-sheet` / `dark-planner-sheet`): the sheet that rises over the hero; also the text colour on ink pills.
- **Surfaces** (`surface`, `surface-2`, `surface-3` and dark counterparts): raised cards, dialogs and menus (`surface`); input wells, callouts, option rows, the checks panel and the empty state (`surface-2`); tags, skeleton peaks and hovered option rows (`surface-3`).
- **Fills** (`--fill`, `--fill-2`: ink at 7% / 12%, 9% / 15% in dark): the resting and hovered fill of every default and quiet control, the season track, the count chip and the add buttons, so a control is a step of ink over whatever it sits on.
- **Hairlines** (`hairline`, `hairline-strong`): the calendar's day columns, hour rules and day-head edge, input and select wells, and unchecked swatches. Nothing else is divided by a line.
- **Ink** (`ink`, `ink-2`, `ink-3`): text, primary buttons, the brand mark, the season thumb, focus rings. Ink-3 is the floor for meta text.

### Course palette
Nine vivid hues assigned per course by default, cycling without repeats among selected courses: cobalt, hot pink, tangerine, violet, aqua, lemon, sky, orchid, periwinkle. Blocks use the hue at full strength with a lit top lip: a crisp 1px white top highlight over a white gradient that is gone 9px down, above the text (the narrowest lanes start their glyphs 8px down, where every hue still clears 4.5:1), because a taller sheen behind white text pushed cobalt (light) and dark violet below 4.5:1. In dark mode the hue is mixed 90% with the surface. Text on a block is ink or white, whichever gives higher WCAG contrast against the fill it sits on: the hue in light mode, the 90% mix in dark mode. In the 3D hero the same hues are darkened to 90% lightness under a full clearcoat; as the board lands, each slab top shifts to its block's exact flat fill so the hand-over is seamless. An empty week shows chrome sample slabs.

### Semantic
- **Success** (passed course), **Warning** (needs review), **Danger** (overlap). Each has a soft companion at 7-12% alpha for callout fills.

### Named Rules
**The One Neon Rule.** Hot pink is the only accent and it appears as small marks (a full stop, a day pill, a count, a hover), never as a button fill or a section background. Primary actions are ink.

**The Colour Is the Course Rule.** Saturation belongs to the course palette. Chrome stays grey and ink; no course is ever grey, and no course hue is pure green, amber or red, so courses can never be mistaken for a state. A selected rail row borrows its course's hue as an 11% wash, the one place chrome takes a course colour, so the list and the week read as one.

**The Never Colour Alone Rule.** Every semantic state carries a second signal: passed is a filled green swatch plus a check icon; review is a dashed amber outline plus a hatched strip; overlap is a solid red outline plus a warning icon.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Geologica (self-hosted Greek and Latin subsets, variable 300-800), with Noto Sans and system-ui as fallback (Noto Sans is only named in the stack; it is not bundled)
**Body Font:** Geologica
**Label/Mono Font:** Geologica; a monospace face appears only in the transfer-code textarea

**Character:** One geometric grotesk with Greek built in, used at every size. Display sizes are heavy-ish and tightly tracked; working sizes stay plain and small, so the numbers and the headline do the shouting.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (620, clamp(52px, 7.2vw, 96px), 0.98, -0.04em): the two-line hero headline only, revealed line by line from masks, each line coming into focus from an 8px blur, and pulled 0.07em left so the capital's side bearing lines up with the sub and the CTA. **Display phone** takes over at 860px and below.
- **Stat** (560, clamp(40px, 4.4vw, 66px), 1.2, -0.04em): the four week totals, rendered as odometer digits; **Stat phone** (38px) at 860px and below.
- **Headline** (600, 24px, 1.2, -0.03em): dialog titles.
- **Title large** (600, 22px, 1.2, -0.025em): the empty-state title.
- **Title** (600, 20px, 1.25, -0.025em): panel headings (course rail, weekly schedule).
- **Title small** (600, 15px, 1.3): the checks summary and dialog subheads.
- **Lede** (400, clamp(16px, 1.3vw, 19px), 1.5): the hero sub-sentence, held to 31ch.
- **Body** (400, 14px, 1.5; paragraphs 1.6): default text with tabular figures.
- **Control** (550, 13px, 1.3): buttons, selects, menu items, the season switch.
- **Event title** (600, 13px, 1.25): course names inside blocks; time lines 12px 600, rooms and teachers 12px. Narrow lanes set the course code at 12px, step it to 11px at 60px or less (the one place text goes under 12px, because the lane has no room) and run it down the lane at 34px or less.
- **Meta** (400, 12.5px): course meta, sync time, notes, tooltips; footers 13px.
- **Label** (600, 12px): tags, badges, counters, day-head meta, hour axis. Sentence case, never uppercase. The brand mark's two-line monogram (11px 700) is a logo, not text, and is the only other exception to the 12px floor.
- **Print ramp** (print only): title 22px (followed by the season and the data's date at 13px in ink-3), stats 20px, day names 11px, event titles 9px, event times, rooms and day meta 8px. Teachers are dropped.

### Named Rules
**The Numbers Are the Headline Rule.** Figures are tabular everywhere, and the week's totals are set at display scale, not tucked into small chips.

**The One Family Rule.** Geologica carries every role; hierarchy comes from size, weight and tracking, never from a second display face.

## Layout

A week-first canvas inside a sheet. The hero is a full-viewport sticky studio (100svh, min 600px; 92svh on phones); the planner sheet overlaps it by 13svh (9svh on phones) and carries everything else. Content tracks a 2080px maximum with fluid 2vw gutters (16px on phones).

- **Desktop (>1180px):** a 340px sticky course rail beside the week, parted from it by 32px of space. On wide layouts the rail can be hidden so the week runs full-width, with the floating rail button to bring it back.
- **Tablet (861-1180px):** the week takes the full width; the rail becomes a 340px side drawer floating 12px from the edges. From 961px the open drawer pushes the week aside rather than covering it, so an added course is seen landing, and pressing or focusing the week leaves the drawer open; below 961px it overlays. It slides in over 500ms and out in 200ms, and tabbing out of it closes it.
- **Phone (<=860px):** the week becomes a vertical day-by-day agenda with sticky day headings; the rail becomes a bottom sheet (84dvh, grab handle, scrim; up in 500ms, down in 200ms) that a finger can drag down from its handle or heading, closing past 80px or on a quick downward flick (over 0.5px/ms at release) and otherwise springing back; dialogs become bottom sheets; the sheet corners drop to the phone sheet radius.
- A floating ink "Μαθήματα" pill with the selected count opens the rail from anywhere once the planner is in view.

Day headings (name, meta, today pill) are centred in their columns and reserve exactly two lines of meta (2.7em) so every day name aligns. Today's meta puts "Σήμερα" on its own line above "N συναντήσεις · N ώρες". On the phone agenda, headings are a left-aligned row and the word runs inline with a comma. Phones and print drop the two-line reservation.

Spacing runs on 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 22 / 28px; panels breathe at 22px vertical padding, the hour grid is 56px per hour, and the header bar is 76px (68px on phones).

### Named Rules
**The No Sideways Week Rule.** The week never scrolls horizontally. The grid is a 54px hour axis (headed Ώρα) plus five equal day columns, whatever they hold. Parallel lessons split their day into lanes, and narrow lanes step down by container width. At 112px or less a lane shows its start time and the name, set compact with soft hyphens at Greek syllable breaks; wider lanes never hyphenate and wrap whole words. At 80px or less, and for lessons of an hour or less, it shows start time plus course code. At 60px or less it shows the code only. At 50px or less the shared ECE prefix drops and only the course number remains. The full name always stays in the tooltip and dialog. Margin notes move beside the week only when the busiest day still gets 150px per lane. There the checks are a sticky margin note on a `surface-2` panel that mirrors the rail, always open, with their summary as a heading rather than a toggle (out of the tab order).

**The Print Is Its Own Layout Rule.** Print is one A4 landscape sheet in the selected theme: chrome hidden, the sheet and blocks exactly as on screen (light or dark), no today marking, the title followed by the season and the data's date, the week scaled to fit and forced fully visible, the print type ramp, all motion, shadows and focus rings off. Nothing on the printed page sizes itself with viewport units, because Chrome's print preview resolves them against the browser window and a window-tall page spills a blank second sheet.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat at rest: controls, callouts, rows and panels part by tone, with no shadow and no outline. Only surfaces that really float take the soft layered shadows and a 1px top highlight in light mode; on ink, where shadows vanish, a hairline marks their edge instead. Real depth lives in the hero, where a physically lit 3D board sits on a shadow-catching floor. Lighting is tuned per theme: in light mode a strong key from the upper left with a soft rim, in dark mode a softer key with a stronger cool rim. The floor shadow is 24% in light mode and 60% in dark mode. The board carries hairline edge lines. In light mode it is near-white and clearcoated; in dark mode it is graphite, rougher, with little clearcoat. The 3D stage is its own fixed layer above the planner sheet. It is cut away over the sheet's toolbar and stats band and over the docked rail, so the board slides under the planner's chrome; the band's lower edge is a 20px fade rather than a hard line, so slabs surface from under the toolbar. There is no glass: the header is a plain bar and the hero tooltip a solid `surface` card with the lifted shadow. The dialog backdrop is the one frost (8px blur).

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Rest** (`0 1px 2px #0e0e0e12, 0 1px 1px #0e0e0e0a`): event blocks only (they are objects on the board).
- **Lifted** (`0 12px 28px -12px #0e0e0e2e, 0 2px 6px -2px #0e0e0e14`): hovered event blocks, the hero tooltip, a block in flight.
- **Floating** (`0 32px 64px -24px #0e0e0e40, 0 6px 16px -6px #0e0e0e17`): dialogs, menus, the rail drawer, the rail button, the update toast.
- **Highlight** (`inset 0 1px 0 #ffffffcc`): paired with floating surfaces.
- **Block lip:** the event blocks' white top sheen (light `#ffffff2e`, dark `#ffffff1c`), gone 9px down.
- **Sheet rise** (`0 -30px 60px -34px #0e0e0e59`): under the planner sheet's top edge where it overlaps the hero.

### Named Rules
**The Press, Don't Lift Rule.** Controls press (scale 0.96 on active) and never rise on hover. Only event blocks lift (1px, over 150ms) on hover, because they are objects on the board, and only where a fine pointer can hover ((hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)), so a tap never leaves a block stuck raised.

**The Rings Inside Rule.** State and highlight outlines on event blocks sit inside the block (negative offset), never outside it. Keyboard focus is the one ring outside: the 2px ink outline offset 2px, raised above the neighbouring lanes. A block with a state ring keeps it, and its focus ring is a shadow outside the block (a 2px sheet gap, then 2px ink), so a focused clash still shows red.

**The Hairline in the Dark Rule.** In dark mode every floating surface (dialog, menu, tooltip, drawer) gets a 1px hairline edge, since shadows cannot read on ink.

## Shapes

Two shape families and nothing between them: everything pressable or countable is a full pill (999px), including buttons, inputs, selects, tags, badges, swatches, the season track and the brand mark; containers are soft rectangles stepping 28px (planner sheet, dialogs; the phone sheet radius on phones, including the rail bottom sheet), 20px (menus, drawer, empty state), 14px (callouts, tooltips, option rows), 10px (menu items). Event blocks sit at 12px, both on the 2D week and as rounded 3D slabs. Course state swatches are 12px circles: filled when selected, a thick ring when some meetings are hidden, dashed when under review.

## Components

### Buttons
Pills that press, never lift.
- **Shape:** full pill (999px), 38px minimum height (44px under a coarse pointer), no border.
- **Default:** a flat `--fill` (hover `--fill-2`), no shadow, 13px weight 550 label with an optional 16px Tabler stroke icon. The refresh button in the sheet's toolbar is a default pill at 44px, not ink.
- **Primary:** flat ink fill with sheet-coloured text; with a fine pointer, hover mixes 20% neon pink into the ink.
- **Quiet:** transparent; hover is the `--fill` wash.
- **Hero CTA and rail button:** 52px ink pills; with a fine pointer the hero CTA leans up to 6px toward a pointer within 80px and springs back on leave (not under reduced motion); the rail button carries a pink count badge and rises in only once the sheet docks. Both take the primary hover.
- **Coloured hovers** (the pink mix on ink pills, the pink and red fills on a rail row's add button) apply only under (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine), so a tap never leaves one stuck; neutral washes hover everywhere.
- **Active / Focus:** scale 0.96 on press; a 2px ink focus outline offset 2px, the same on event blocks, checkboxes and the theme select.

### Chips
- **Tags and badges:** pills on `surface-3` with ink-2 text at 12px weight 600; success, warning and danger variants use the soft fill and the state colour with an icon.
- **Credit tag:** a small ink pill (for ECTS) on each course row.
- **Legend items:** pills on `surface-2` with an 18px colour swatch that doubles as a colour picker. Coloured by course, a chip shows the course code only (the name is its tooltip and the picker's label; the printed legend names each course in full); the legend bar is hidden while no course is selected.

### Cards / Containers
- **Corner Style:** 28px for the sheet and dialogs, 20px for menus and the drawer, 14px for callouts.
- **Background:** `surface` for raised, `surface-2` for inset callouts and source rows.
- **Shadow Strategy:** floating plus highlight for surfaces that float (see Elevation); none for inset containers.

- **Empty state:** an inset `surface-2` panel (20px corners, no border, no wash) with a tiny week glyph on a small flat `surface` card: five 16px day columns, one 3px-cornered block placed in each at its own hour (one of them pink, today), dropping in from above one after another, above the title and two lines of help.
- **Dialogs in motion:** they rise in on the ease-out (280ms from 10px lower at 0.97 scale, the backdrop fading in over 200ms; phone bottom sheets slide up in 380ms on `cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1)`). Closing, whether by a close button, Escape or a finished action, runs back the way they came: a fade with an 8px drop and 0.98 scale over 160ms on the ease-out, the frosted backdrop fading with it; phone bottom sheets slide back down (200ms). Instant under reduced motion.
- **Update toast:** when a new version is published (sw.js has already cached its files), a pill on `surface` with the floating shadow floats 10px under the header: "Υπάρχει νέα έκδοση." with a primary "Ανανέωση" and a quiet close. It eases in from above (280ms on the ease-out, from 10px higher at 0.96 scale) and, when closed, leaves upward the way it came (200ms); print and the HTML backup leave it out.
- **Update report:** after a refresh or an import, a collapsed "Τι άλλαξε · N αλλαγές" card on `surface-2` sits right under the notice and lists what changed against the previous data, grouped as changed, added and removed meetings and catalog changes (season, code and name in ink, the detail in ink-2). The next notice hides it; print and the HTML backup leave it out.

### Hero Week (scroll morph)
The morph follows the scroll on a critically damped spring rather than 1:1: a slow scroll is tracked closely, while a fast or back-and-forth one plays at the motion's own pace (about four tenths of a second end to end, no reversal on a jitter) and always ends where the page is. The crossfade to the planner's own blocks is eased too, so it never pops when a deal or a throw ends.

A Three.js board (near-white or graphite, with hairline edges) carrying the student's courses as glossy slabs, or chrome slabs while the week is empty. Its five equal day columns match the DOM grid. Slab height encodes how often a course meets per week: 0.18 world units plus 0.22 per extra weekly meeting, capped at five. A once-a-week course is nearly flat; three or more meetings stand clearly tall. The sample week varies by its own counts. Each real slab has its course code engraved, centred, into its top: a groove in the slab's own colour, darker the deeper it goes, matte with no clearcoat, and walls drawn as a normal map from the lettering so they catch the scene's light and shift as the board turns. Narrow tops shrink the code, dropping the ECE prefix last; a top too small stays plain. The board marks hours with lines and half hours with fainter dashed lines; day names shrink to fit their column, never grow. All words on the board (day labels, hours, engraved codes) fade together over e .35-.65, before the planner's own text arrives. Loading: while the data loads, the planner shows shimmering skeletons of its course rows, stats, day heads and blocks; until the 3D board is live, the hero shows a skeleton of the board in its pose (the same shimmer on a plane in the surface colour), which fades as the board rises in, and only where the 3D can load (html.has-3d), so it never waits forever. Rendering budget: while anything moves, the slow drift and conflict pulse included, the board draws on every display refresh (144 times a second on a 144 Hz monitor), with the shadow pass resting while only the drift moves (under reduced motion each still frame draws its shadows, since a board drawn before its shadow map renders invisible); once the drift has eased out it draws nothing until the next input, and docked under the planner it draws nothing until the page scrolls back to the docking point (a 1px marker just above it comes into view; hidden in print and removed from the HTML backup), a resize or the drawer closing wakes it. It starts in short tasks with shaders compiled asynchronously, and canvases behind uploaded textures are freed. In the hero it drifts gently (easing out 15s after the last input, when the loop sleeps until the next one), follows the cursor, carries a soft shadowless light that trails a fine pointer across the board on a spring (so the glossy slabs catch a moving sheen; gone before the landing, never on touch or under reduced motion), shows a tooltip on hover and opens the course dialog on click. On load the slabs are dealt onto the rising board Monday to Friday, 40ms apart (from 520ms, at most sixteen steps): each falls from above, accelerating under gravity, and squashes as it lands (the squash springs back), so no slab sinks into the board. A rebuild (a course added or removed, a season switch) shrinks the old slabs away and deals the new ones the same way.

**Free view.** The hero board can be explored by hand:
- **Orbit:** the board behaves like a weighted object. Grabbing it catches and stops a spin. While held it follows the hand on a stiff spring, with a slight lag and overshoot. Released, it keeps the hand's speed, soft-limited so a heavy board never whips round: a harder push still goes further, but even a violent flick turns it less than half a turn. It then comes to a firm stop under drag plus a constant grip, in under a second, instead of creeping. Yaw is free. Past its pitch band, tilt stretches like a rubber band and springs back, and the camera never flips over the top.
- **Feel:** while held the board lifts slightly, then drops back on release with one small settle, not a bounce train. It banks into a spin and nods with a tilt. Slabs lean like jelly, more for taller and outer slabs, and squash as the board lands. All of this fades out through the morph. Under reduced motion a drag sets the pose directly: no throw, lift, settle or jelly.
- **Zoom:** pinch or Ctrl+wheel zooms between 0.8x and 1.6x. A plain wheel always scrolls the page.
- **Reset and cursors:** double-click resets the view. The cursor is grab, then grabbing while dragging.
- **Touch:** horizontal swipes spin the board and vertical swipes scroll.
- **Click vs drag:** a 5px threshold separates the two, so a click on a slab still opens the course.
- **Spin too fast:** each slab has a grip of 600 on ω²·(r+1.5)·(1+0.8h), where ω is the spin in rad/s, r the slab's distance from the board's centre and h its height. A slab lets go only while the board is held and spun by hand, after 0.25s continuously over its grip, so the rim and the tall slabs go first and a flick or a released throw never spills anything. Roughly: a brisk drag of about 800px/s keeps every slab, about 1400px/s held for 0.25s spills the rim, and a full-width hard swipe clears the board. A slab that lets go leaves along the spin with a small upward pop, tumbles, and is just fast enough to clear the board's edge. It lands on the studio floor in a ring in view, bounces once, slides to a stop and rolls flat, top up. Slabs on the floor nudge apart rather than overlap. Free slabs live in a layer that turns with the camera, so they rest on a studio floor that stays still while the board spins. Once every flung slab has landed and the spin has calmed (below 1.2 rad/s), they hop back one by one, Monday to Friday, 40ms apart: each a single 420-560ms arc to its exact slot, landing with a squash. If the page scrolls, they all hop home at once, 18ms apart.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** make primary actions ink pills; keep hot pink to small marks (full stop, today, counts, hover).
- **Do** give every pressable thing a full pill shape and the 0.96 press.
- **Do** let free-view interactions (orbit, zoom) yield to scroll and to the copy: plain wheel scrolls, the board springs home on scroll, and it never covers the headline.
- **Do** pick block text colour by contrast (ink or white) for any course hue, including user-chosen ones, measured against the fill the text sits on (the dark-theme mix in dark mode).
- **Do** pair every success, warning and danger state with an icon, dashes or an outline.
- **Do** use the spring easing (`linear()` with ~5% overshoot, fallback cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)) for things that settle into a new value or slot (digits, the season thumb, a block shifting lanes), the ease-out (cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)) for the 2D press-in and for exits, and the there-and-back curve (cubic-bezier(.77,0,.175,1)) only for one-shot pulses that return to rest; make every animation, view transition, smooth scroll and 3D loop disappear under reduced motion.
- **Do** deal tiles and blocks onto the week Monday to Friday (by day, then start time), 40ms apart and the whole deal held to about half a second (bigger weeks close the gap), with one landing per change: in 3D a fall that accelerates under gravity and ends in a squash, never a spring that overshoots into the board; in 2D a press from scale 1.06 to size about the block's centre, with a fade and the lifted shadow coming down to rest (220ms on the ease-out). While the 3D week stands in for the planner's blocks, the change lands in 3D alone.
- **Do** make exits shorter than their entrances (160-200ms) and send things out the way they came in, on the ease-out; the one exception is a removed block, whose opacity runs on the CSS `ease` keyword so the fade stays readable while its scale stays on the ease-out. For the opposite direction use mirrored keyframes, never `animation-direction: reverse`, which turns the ease-out into an ease-in.
- **Do** keep five equal day columns and step narrow lanes down (time and name, time and code, code, number) rather than widening a busy day.
- **Do** keep the week fully visible without horizontal scroll at every width, and keep print as a dedicated A4 landscape layout.
- **Do** keep the 3D week and the planner's week the same object: same course colours, the same five equal columns and lanes, one hand-over sequence with never two hour axes on screen.
- **Do** use tabular figures and display-scale numerals for the week's totals.

### Don't:
- **Don't** add a second accent colour or fill buttons, panels or headers with neon pink.
- **Don't** use grey, pure green, amber or red as a course colour.
- **Don't** introduce a second typeface or uppercase tracked labels; Geologica in sentence case carries every role.
- **Don't** use square or small-radius buttons; nothing pressable sits between pill and rectangle.
- **Don't** lift controls on hover or use hard offset shadows; controls press, only event blocks lift.
- **Don't** draw state or highlight rings outside an event block.
- **Don't** tint the neutrals cool or warm; the studio greys are neutral.
